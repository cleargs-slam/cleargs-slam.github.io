Abstract

We present ClearGS-SLAM, an RGB-D SLAM system designed to handle environments containing transparent objects. Recent GS-based SLAM systems implicitly assume that all observed surfaces are opaque. When transparent objects are present, depth sensors return missing or background measurements on their surfaces, and the reconstruction tends to lose them, which degrades both camera tracking and mapping.

We address this problem by explicitly handling transparency in both tracking and mapping. We employ a monocular network to predict transparent-object masks and depth. Guided by the masks, we exclude depth residuals in transparent regions from camera tracking, thereby mitigating the impact of unreliable depth measurements on transparent surfaces. For mapping, we align the predicted depth to the metric scale of the sensor depth and fuse it with the raw depth, using the resulting fused depth for geometric supervision, while explicitly representing transparent surfaces with low-opacity Gaussians.

Extensive experiments show that ClearGS-SLAM maintains competitive trajectory accuracy compared with state-of-the-art SLAM systems, while achieving the best rendering quality and geometric reconstruction accuracy of transparent regions.

Method

ClearGS-SLAM takes RGB-D frames as input and jointly estimates the camera poses and a 3D Gaussian map. A frozen monocular network predicts a transparent-object mask and a depth map from each RGB frame, and the three components below decide how these predictions are used.

Fused Depth Estimation

The predicted depth is defined only up to an unknown scale and shift, so it cannot be combined with the sensor depth directly. We align it to the metric scale of the raw depth with RANSAC, using only pixels outside the transparent mask where the RGB-D sensor is reliable, and reject clearly inconsistent alignments. The fused depth then replaces the raw depth only inside the mask and keeps the sensor measurements everywhere else.

From left to right: raw RGB, raw depth, predicted transparent mask, predicted depth, scale-aligned depth, and the fused depth. The transparent objects missing from the raw depth are filled in at the correct metric distance.

Mask-Guided Tracking

On a transparent surface the color observation remains informative, whereas the sensor depth is either invalid or belongs to the background behind the object. The pose is therefore estimated from the RGB residual over the whole image and the depth residual only outside the transparent mask. Keyframe candidates with strong motion blur are rejected with a Laplacian sharpness test, since blur destabilizes both the mask prediction and the map update.

Transparent-Region Gaussian Mapping

Back-projecting the raw depth inside the mask would place Gaussians on the background seen through the object. Transparent-region Gaussians are instead initialized from the fused depth and given a lower initial opacity and a relaxed pruning threshold, so that a visible background contribution is retained and they are not removed before their color and geometry are optimized. Online mapping uses the fused depth as geometric supervision, with a reduced weight inside the mask, and the map is globally refined after online SLAM with the estimated trajectory fixed.
